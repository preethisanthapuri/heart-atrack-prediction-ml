# Phase 14 — Ablation and subgroup analysis

## Experimental design

The experiment used the 241 rows in the saved primary training partition. The 61-row holdout partition was excluded. Six staged configurations were compared with identical five-fold stratified outer splits and pooled out-of-fold (OOF) predictions. Preprocessing and feature selection were refit inside folds. Hyperparameter selection used three-fold CV contained in the corresponding outer training fold. Probability threshold 0.5 is shown only as a conventional coding point for descriptive metrics; it is not a medical threshold.

| Stage | Configuration |
|---|---|
| A | Logistic Regression, all encoded inputs |
| B | Logistic Regression with fold-local mutual-information selection, k=15 |
| C | B plus inner-CV selection of Logistic Regression C from 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10 |
| D | Phase 9 soft vote: Logistic Regression, KNN, Random Forest |
| E | D with sigmoid `CalibratedClassifierCV` fit inside each outer training fold |
| F | MI-selected/tuned Logistic Regression plus tuned KNN and Random Forest soft vote, followed by sigmoid calibration on a disjoint calibration subset |

For F, each outer training fold was separated into a development subset and a disjoint 20% calibration subset. Tuning and model fitting were restricted to development rows; the sigmoid mapping was fitted on calibration scores; the outer fold was used only for scoring. This conservative split means F uses fewer model-fit rows per fold than A–E. All metrics treat dataset code `1` as positive; the clinical label meaning has not been independently confirmed.

## Interpretation

The integrated F configuration did not improve the overall results: its pooled accuracy, ROC-AUC, average precision, and Brier score were worse than A and B. The selected-feature B configuration had the highest ROC-AUC and average precision and lowest Brier score, while A had the highest accuracy and F1. E had the highest class-1 recall at the illustrative 0.5 cutoff, paired with lower class-0 specificity than A. These differences are descriptive on a small single-dataset training partition; no significance claim is made. The evidence does not justify presenting the full integrated ensemble as a superior proposed framework. Feature selection is a candidate for further independent evaluation, not established improvement.

## Subgroup analysis

Descriptive subgroup metrics use Stage F OOF probabilities for training rows only. Group fields are reported as `sex_code` because source provenance and codebook have not been confirmed. Age bands (<50, 50–59, 60–69, 70+) are descriptive bins. For `sex_code=0` (n=76) and `sex_code=1` (n=165), respectively, class-1 recall was 0.964 and 0.855; class-0 specificity was 0.714 and 0.764; accuracy was 0.895 and 0.806. For age bands with n=71, 98, 63, and 9, recall was 0.959, 0.863, 0.840, and 1.000; specificity was 0.636, 0.766, 0.816, and 0.667. The `70+` group is especially sparse and contains only three class-0 examples.

These disparities are unstable descriptive estimates, not a fairness certification or evidence of discriminatory impact. The target label semantics are unresolved, sample sizes are small, and no uncertainty intervals or demographic codebook are available. No subgroup threshold adjustment was attempted. Complete subgroup metrics and metric ranges are in `results/tables/fairness.csv` and `results/tables/fairness_disparity.csv`.

## Limitations

All six stages share one dataset and the same outer folds; this is a controlled internal comparison but not an independent replication. Hyperparameter selection has residual selection optimism even though evaluation occurs on outer folds. Stage F's calibration subset reduces fitting data. Group comparisons are not adjusted for covariates and have no inferential intervals. The prior Phase 13 source audit found uncertain dataset provenance and a target-code direction discrepancy relative to the external source. Therefore none of these values support clinical risk, heart-attack diagnosis, or clinical fairness claims.
